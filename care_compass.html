How PCOS is Clinically Evaluated (Rotterdam 2003 / 2023 Guidelines)

PCOS cannot be diagnosed by an AI algorithm or ultrasound image alone. Under international consensus guidelines, a formal diagnosis requires meeting at least two out of three criteria after excluding other mimicking etiologies (such as congenital adrenal hyperplasia, thyroid disorders, and hyperprolactinemia):

Criterion 1
Ovulatory Dysfunction

Oligomenorrhea (cycles > 35 days or < 8 cycles per year) or amenorrhea (absence of menses > 90 days).

Criterion 2
Hyperandrogenism

Clinical signs (hirsutism, persistent cystic acne, androgenic alopecia) or elevated serum androgens (Total/Free Testosterone, FAI, DHEAS).

Criterion 3
Polycystic Ovarian Morphology

>= 20 follicles (2–9 mm) per ovary, or ovarian volume >= 10 mL on high-resolution transvaginal ultrasonography.
